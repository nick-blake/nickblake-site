SPARK

Helping Shake Shack General Managers turn data into next steps

Shake Shack · Product Designer · AI-powered restaurant tool (MVP)

  • ~20 min across 3 tools → 1 screen
  • 65%of suggested actions accepted
  • 10 of 10GMs wanted to keep using the tool

My role

I was the sole product designer on SPARK. I owned the design end to end: GM research, journey mapping, the product design, and the SPARK design system. I worked with a product manager and an offshore engineering team.

The problem

GMs spent about 20 minutes jumping between tools before they could fix anything.

General Managers are accountable for hospitality scores on every shift. But the data they need lives in three different tools, and none of them say what to do next.

I interviewed GMs and mapped their journey to find where the time went:

  • Noticing a problem: they walked the floor. I find out when the line’s out the door.
  • Snowflake: slow reports, no live view. I have to dig to find the right report.
  • Tableau: survey scores lagged behind the shift. By the time I see it, lunch is over.
  • Harri: the schedule didn’t connect to wait times. I’m flipping between screens.
  • Deciding: still no clear priority. Just tell me what to fix first.

By GMs’ own estimates, it took about 20 minutes to get from noticing a problem to acting on it.

The hypothesis

GMs didn’t need more data. They needed to know what to do next.

Shake Shack wanted better hospitality scores across its restaurants. My hypothesis was that if insights and next steps lived in one place, GMs would act faster and more often.

I defined success as:

  • Less time from insight to action
  • Suggested actions accepted
  • High GM satisfaction

The user

Designed for a GM on the floor, mid-shift, with seconds to spare.

GMs aren’t at a desk. They glance at a screen between tasks while running the shift. Every screen had to answer “what’s wrong and what do I do?” in one look.

Constraints

Each constraint shaped how I worked.

  • MVP scope → hospitality only. The full vision covered labor, inventory and team too. I cut the MVP to one area so we could prove the core idea fast and with less risk.
  • An offshore dev team → prototype fast. With limited overlap across time zones, I used Figma MCP and AI tools to prototype quickly, so ideas moved from design to working software faster.
  • No existing design system → I built one alongside the product. Status colors (on, near and off goal), an Inter type scale, and reusable metric and action cards kept the build consistent and make it easy to add labor or inventory next.

Iterations

Three rounds, each fixing what the last got wrong: what → why → can I trust it.

Iteration one: metrics first

Actions and metrics sat side by side, with nothing connecting them.

Suggested actions were ranked by severity, meaning how far each metric was from its goal. But nothing linked an action to the metric behind it. Open a second register sat next to six metric cards, and GMs had to figure out which number it was responding to. So they went back to the metrics to work it out themselves, the same cross-referencing SPARK was supposed to end.

Iteration two: actions with context

Each action now has two parts: the recommendation, and the driver behind it.

The recommendation says what to do: Open a second register until 1:30 PM. The driver explains why, using live data: Wait time is 6:40, 33% over goal, and the line is still growing. With the reason built into each action, GMs no longer needed the metrics to decide. So actions moved to the top, and the metrics moved to a secondary position as supporting evidence.

Iteration three: show the source (selected)

GMs won’t act on AI they can’t check, so every action names its source.

Even with a reason attached, GMs hesitated. They’re accountable for every shift, and before SPARK they’d always pulled the numbers themselves. So each action now shows where its data comes from, like Snowflake or Harri, the tools they already trusted. A familiar source gave them the confidence to act.

Results

In a 1-month pilot across 10 restaurants, GMs went from ~20 minutes across 3 tools to one screen, and every one of them wanted to keep SPARK.

  • ~20 minutes across 3 tools → 1 screen
  • 65% of suggested actions accepted. GMs followed SPARK’s recommendation about two out of three times.
  • 10 of 10 GMs wanted to keep using SPARK after the pilot

Reflection

People act on AI they can verify.

When AI makes a recommendation, it has to show where the data comes from. A clear source is what turns a suggestion into something people trust enough to act on. It’s how I now approach every AI product I design.

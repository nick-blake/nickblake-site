SPARK

Helping Shake Shack General Managers turn data into next steps

Shake Shack · Senior UX Designer · AI-powered restaurant tool (MVP)

  • 20 min → secondsfrom insight to action
  • 65%of suggested actions accepted
  • 10 of 10GMs wanted to keep using it

The problem

General Managers are accountable for hospitality scores on every shift, but the data they need is spread across several tools. Before a GM could act on anything, they had to check them all.

We interviewed GMs, then recorded and transcribed the sessions with Otter AI. I mapped their journey to find where the time went:

  • Noticing a problem. They usually found issues by walking the floor. I find out when the line’s out the door.
  • Snowflake. Reports were slow and there was no live view. I have to dig to find the right report.
  • Tableau. Survey scores lagged behind the shift. By the time I see it, lunch is over.
  • Harri. The schedule didn’t connect to wait times. I’m flipping between screens.
  • Deciding. Even after all that, they still weren’t sure what to fix first. Just tell me what to fix first.

By GMs’ own estimates, it took about 20 minutes to get from noticing a problem to acting on it. During a lunch rush, that’s too slow.

Goals

Business need: improve hospitality scores across restaurants.

Hypothesis: if insights and next steps live in one place, GMs will act faster and more often.

Success criteria:

  • Reduce the time from insight to action
  • Drive the acceptance rate of suggested actions
  • Achieve high GM satisfaction

The user

GMs are on the floor during shifts, not at a desk. They piece together data from Snowflake, Tableau and Harri, and they’re accountable for hospitality scores on every shift. Anything I designed had to be readable at a glance and usable mid-shift.

Constraints

  • MVP scope. The full vision covered hospitality, labor, inventory and team. We cut the MVP to hospitality only so we could prove the core idea first.
  • Offshore dev team. I had to make handoff clear enough to work across time zones without constant back-and-forth.
  • A design system built alongside the product. I created the SPARK design system as we went. It included status colors for on, near and off goal, a simple Inter type scale, and core components like metric cards and action cards. This kept the team consistent and made the product easy to extend later.

Iterations

Iteration one: metrics first

The dashboard led with metric cards showing how today compared with goal, and suggested actions sat below. GMs could see what was off, but they still asked, So what do I do?

Iteration two: actions first

I flipped the hierarchy. Recommended actions moved to the top, ranked by impact on guests, and each one came with its reason. For example: Open a second register until 1:30 PM. Wait time is 6:40, 33% over goal, and the line is still growing. GMs no longer had to cross-reference metrics to understand why.

Iteration three: show the source (selected)

Each action now names where its data comes from, such as Snowflake or Harri. Seeing the source gave GMs enough trust in a recommendation to act on it.

Results

  • 20 minutes → seconds from insight to action
  • 65% of suggested actions accepted
  • 10 of 10 GMs wanted to keep using SPARK

Reflection

People act on AI they can verify. The biggest jump didn’t come from smarter recommendations. It came from showing GMs where each recommendation came from.

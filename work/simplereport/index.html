SimpleReport

Helping health workers send rapid test results to the CDC

Skylight · Senior Product Designer · Self-serve device management

  • 2 days → minutesto enable a new condition
  • 75%fewer support tickets
  • 12% → 52%of reporting beyond COVID-19

The problem

SimpleReport started as a COVID-19 reporting tool. When testing sites wanted to report on other conditions, like flu or RSV, they couldn’t turn them on themselves. They had to contact support and wait, often for about two days, before they could start testing.

I mapped the experience across the three user roles (org admins, standard users and entry-only users) to find what was blocking them, from setup through registering patients, running tests and managing patient info:

  • Setup. Enabling a new condition took about two days and depended on support. I don’t like that I need to wait on customer support.
  • Registering patients. Entering patient data was slow and redundant. It takes a while to get started testing.
  • Conducting tests. Staff weren’t confident explaining data privacy. How do I explain to patients that the data is safe?
  • Managing patients. Dense forms slowed down editing. The number of fields is overwhelming.

This case study focuses on the biggest blocker: setup.

Goals

Business need: $32M was invested to expand SimpleReport beyond COVID-19.

Hypothesis: if health admins can quickly enable additional diseases, the CDC will receive a higher volume and range of test results.

Success criteria:

  • Reduce the time to enable a new condition
  • Reduce support tickets
  • Increase adoption beyond COVID-19

The users

Nurses use SimpleReport repeatedly over a full shift. Turnover is high, so there’s no time for training. Speed and clarity matter above everything.

Health administrators manage settings for the entire site. They’re time-poor and juggle multiple responsibilities. They use the tool only occasionally, but they’re the decision-makers.

Constraints

  • A growing device list. Every new test device added to the catalog made the list longer, so the design had to scale.
  • User permissions. Three roles (org admin, standard and entry-only) meant controlling who could change site settings.
  • Self-serve. Admins needed to make changes on their own, without contacting support.

Explorations

Exploration one: a toggle list

Familiar, but every new device makes the list longer.

Exploration two: search and filters

Faster filtering, but the full catalog still lives on the page.

Exploration three (selected): show what the site runs

The page shows only the devices the site actually uses, and the full catalog stays one step away. Validated with 5 admins.

Results

  • 2 days → minutes to enable a condition
  • 75% fewer support tickets
  • 12% → 52% of reporting beyond COVID-19

Reflection

The best proof a design works is the workaround disappearing.
